Italy

3 historic landmarks

The Colosseum

Rome

Inaugurated in 80 AD under Emperor Titus, this 50,000-seat amphitheater hosted gladiatorial combats, wild animal hunts, and public spectacles that bound Roman citizens to imperial power. Its trapdoors and vaulted arches still shape stadium engineering.

The Pantheon

Rome

Rebuilt by Emperor Hadrian around 125 AD as a temple to all Roman gods, this architectural marvel features the world’s largest unreinforced concrete dome, pierced by a central oculus. Converted into a Christian church in 609 AD, its continuous use preserved its ancient interior.

Pompeii Archaeological Park

Campania

When Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD, volcanic ash buried this Roman port city overnight, halting everyday life in mid-motion. Excavated bakeries, frescoed villas, and plaster casts offer an intimate look at ordinary Romans two thousand years ago.

France

3 historic landmarks

Eiffel Tower

Paris

Constructed for the 1889 World’s Fair marking the centenary of the French Revolution, this 330-meter wrought-iron lattice tower was initially mocked by Parisian artists. Its value as a radio telegraphy station helped save it, and it became France’s emblem of modern ingenuity.

Palace of Versailles

Versailles

King Louis XIV transformed a hunting lodge into a baroque palace in the late 17th century, centralizing royal power in its Hall of Mirrors. In 1789, bread riots marched to its gates; in 1919, its halls hosted the treaty ending World War I.

Notre-Dame Cathedral

Paris

Begun in 1163 on the Île de la Cité, this Gothic masterpiece witnessed royal coronations, Napoleon’s self-crowning, and the liberation of Paris in 1944. Its restoration after the April 2019 fire renewed a landmark at the spiritual heart of France.
